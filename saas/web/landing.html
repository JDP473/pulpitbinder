Everything a sermon needs, bound together

Most preachers keep the manuscript in one program, the slides in another, the notes on paper, and the recording somewhere else. Pulpit Binder keeps them together, and keeps them in step.

Write the manuscript

A writing space made for preaching: headings, highlights, pauses, and slide cues. Type a reference and press Tab for a Scripture section in your favorite translations.

Slides that follow along

Bring in your PowerPoint or a PDF and bind each slide to its place in the text. Every Scripture section makes its own passage slides, and any slide can be edited or turned into another kind.

Preach from one screen

The manuscript scrolls and the matching slide stays beside it. On a phone or tablet the text leads and the slides stay small.

Your preaching, at a glance

A dashboard of every sermon: how often and how long, which books of the Bible, which themes and doctrines, and what may be missing.

Study and analysis

Word count, timing, passages cited, recurring words, and voice. Compare what you wrote with what you preached once a recording is in.

On every device

Write on the computer in the study and pick up the tablet on Sunday morning. Your binder stays in step, and works offline at the pulpit once it has loaded.

Claude's help, on your own key

Pulpit Binder can ask Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, for help that saves hours of the week. It is written for preachers in the churches of Christ, and it never writes the sermon for you.

  • A plain read of the sermon's substance: the big idea, how it handles the text, and what may be missing.
  • Suggestions as you write, and an outline drawn from your own manuscript.
  • Slides from the words you highlight: quotes, key verses, a big idea, fill in the blank.
  • Study notes on a passage: cross references, word studies, and questions answered plainly.

How it works

  1. Create an API key in the Anthropic Console and add a little credit.
  2. Paste the key into your Account here. It is kept encrypted and used only for your requests.
  3. Anthropic bills you directly for what you use. For a sermon or two a week, that is usually a dollar or two a month.

Everything else, writing, slides, preaching, the dashboard, and sync, works without a key and costs nothing.

Free to use. Not free to run.

Pulpit Binder is a freely.church project. Like the church directory there, it is free for every preacher, with no tiers and no ads. Those who are able can give to cover the server, storage, and backups, and the time to keep improving it.

Questions

Who is Pulpit Binder for?

Any preacher who writes a manuscript or full notes and preaches with slides. It was built by a pulpit minister for the work of an ordinary week, and opened up so other preachers could use it too.

Do I need an Anthropic key to use it?

No. Writing, Scripture sections you paste in, slides, preaching, the dashboard, and sync all work without one. The key turns on Claude's help: analysis, suggestions, outlines, slide drafts, and passage text.

What does Claude cost?

Anthropic charges by use. A full sermon worked through end to end costs about a quarter on the default setting, so most preachers spend a dollar or two a month. You can choose faster, cheaper answers or more careful ones in Settings.

Is my work private?

Your sermons, slides, and recordings are kept on our server under your account and are not shared with anyone. Claude requests go to Anthropic with your own key, under Anthropic's commercial terms. You can delete your account and everything in it at any time.

Where does the Bible text come from?

You can paste any translation, and Claude can supply the text of a passage when your key is in. Pasted and loaded passages are kept inside the sermon, so they work offline at the pulpit.

Can I use my own PowerPoint?

Yes. Bring in a .pptx and it is shown to match PowerPoint, or bring in a PDF export of any deck. Slides made in Pulpit Binder are styled to match your deck, and you can save the whole deck back out as a PowerPoint.

How is it free?

It is offered freely, as the gospel was freely given to us. Running it costs a little each month, and giving from those who are able keeps it that way for everyone.
